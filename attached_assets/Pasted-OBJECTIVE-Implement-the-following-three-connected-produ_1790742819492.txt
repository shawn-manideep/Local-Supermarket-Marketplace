OBJECTIVE

Implement the following three connected product/customer-commerce enhancements in the existing Local Supermarket Marketplace application:

Change 1 — Product quantity/pack-size variants

Products must support multiple configurable sellable variants such as:

Rice
├── 500 g  — ₹40
├── 1 kg   — ₹75
├── 2 kg   — ₹145
└── 5 kg   — ₹350

Other examples:

Milk
├── 500 ml
└── 1 litre

Eggs
├── 6 pieces
├── 12 pieces
└── 30 pieces

Apple
├── 1 kg
└── 2 kg

Each product must be able to have its own independent set of variants.

Change 2 — Add-to-cart browsing behavior

Currently, adding an item can take the customer toward checkout.

Change this behavior.

After the customer selects a product variant and quantity and taps Add to Cart:

DO NOT navigate directly to Checkout.

The customer must remain on the current shopping screen and continue adding other products.

When the cart contains items, show a persistent/sticky cart action near the bottom of the shopping screen:

┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Go to Cart                 5 Items →│
└─────────────────────────────────────┘

The customer can continue shopping until they explicitly select Go to Cart.

Change 3 — Previous orders + Repeat Order

Customers must be able to see their previous orders and the supermarket from which each order was placed.

Each previous order should provide:

View Order
Repeat Order

When the customer selects Repeat Order, do NOT immediately create a new order.

Instead:

Previous Order
      ↓
Validate current store/product/variant availability
      ↓
Add currently available items to cart
      ↓
Customer reviews cart
      ↓
Checkout
      ↓
New order

The previous order itself must remain unchanged.

Implement a normalized product-variant model.

Recommended table:

product_variants

Relationship:

store
  ↓

  ↓

PRODUCT VARIANT FIELDS

Do not store only a display string such as "500 g" as the authoritative quantity representation.

VARIANT PRICE AND STOCK

Price and stock must belong to the variant, not the parent product.

Example:

Rice
    500 g → ₹40 → stock 20
    1 kg  → ₹75 → stock 15
    2 kg  → ₹145 → stock 10

Do not maintain one authoritative price/stock value on the parent product for variant products.

The backend must treat the variant as the sellable inventory unit.

VARIANT OWNERSHIP

A product variant belongs to the same store as its parent product.

Never allow a vendor to create or modify a variant belonging to another store.

The backend must derive store ownership from authenticated vendor context.

Never trust a client-provided store_id for authorization.

VARIANT SOFT DELETE

Do not hard-delete variants that may have historical references.

Use:

is_active

for discontinued/deactivated variants.

Historical orders must remain readable.

EXISTING PRODUCT MIGRATION

Do not delete existing DEV products.

Existing products must continue working after the migration.

Create an appropriate default variant for existing products where necessary, preserving their current:

price
stock
availability

Do not guess a misleading real-world pack size.

If the existing product does not have enough information to determine a meaningful quantity/unit, use a clearly neutral/default representation rather than inventing a pack size.

Document the migration.

PHASE 4 — CART DATA MODEL

The exact schema may use existing conventions, but product_variant_id must identify the selected sellable variant.

The same variant added twice should increase the existing cart-item quantity rather than create unnecessary duplicate cart rows.

However, different variants of the same product must remain separate cart lines.

Example:
