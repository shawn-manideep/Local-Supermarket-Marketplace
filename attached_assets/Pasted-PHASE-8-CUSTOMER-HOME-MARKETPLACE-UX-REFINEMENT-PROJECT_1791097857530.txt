PHASE 8 — CUSTOMER HOME & MARKETPLACE UX REFINEMENT

PROJECT:
Local Supermarket Marketplace

OBJECTIVE:
Refine the Customer Portal's Home, Marketplace, navigation, discovery, and shopping UX based on the existing approved architecture and previously identified customer-facing issues.

IMPORTANT:
Phases 1–7 are completed and manually accepted.

DO NOT break or redesign the approved functionality.

The following are already accepted and must remain intact:

- Master Catalog
- Vendor product management
- Product variants
- Global customer search
- Product details
- Cart
- Reorder / Buy Again
- Stock management
- Vendor order management
- Delivery Partner workflow
- Customer order status
- Historical order snapshots
- One-store cart rule
- Customer/vendor/partner authorization

This phase is CUSTOMER UX REFINEMENT ONLY.

--------------------------------------------------
1. CURRENT CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE GOALS
--------------------------------------------------

The Customer Portal should make it immediately clear:

1. What supermarkets/stores are available.
2. Which store the customer is currently shopping from.
3. What products are available in that store.
4. What the customer recently ordered.
5. What store the customer recently used.
6. How to search products globally.
7. How to navigate back safely.
8. How to access the cart contextually.
9. How to continue shopping without losing context.

The experience should be simple enough for a normal grocery customer without requiring them to understand the underlying catalog structure.

--------------------------------------------------
2. CUSTOMER HOME
--------------------------------------------------

Review and refine the Customer Home screen.

The Home screen should provide clear sections such as:

- Search
- Available Supermarkets
- Recently Ordered
- Recent Store
- Categories / product discovery
- Relevant shopping content

Do not add unnecessary dashboard-style widgets.

Do not overload the Home screen.

Prioritize shopping.

--------------------------------------------------
3. AVAILABLE SUPERMARKETS
--------------------------------------------------

Customer should be able to discover supermarkets/stores that are currently eligible for shopping.

Use the existing store/pincode eligibility logic.

Do not expose stores that the customer cannot actually shop from.

Each store should communicate enough information for the customer to understand what they are selecting.

Do not expose vendor-private information.

Do not expose internal store IDs.

If there are no eligible stores:

Show a clear empty state.

Example:

"No supermarkets are currently available in your area."

Provide an appropriate retry/change-location/pincode action if the existing application supports it.

--------------------------------------------------
4. RECENT STORE
--------------------------------------------------

Add/refine a "Recent Store" section.

If the customer has previously completed or placed an order from a store, show the most relevant recent store.

The customer should be able to tap the store and return to shopping there.

Do not automatically switch the active store without a clear user action.

If there is no previous store:

Do not show fake data.

Use an appropriate empty state or omit the section.

--------------------------------------------------
5. RECENTLY ORDERED
--------------------------------------------------

Add/refine a "Recently Ordered" section.

Use the customer's actual order history.

Show useful information such as:

- Product
- Variant
- Store
- Current availability
- Current price where appropriate
- Reorder / Buy Again action

Do not use historical price as the current shopping price.

Do not silently substitute another product or variant.

Use the already-approved exact historical identity behavior from Phase 3.

If an item is no longer available:

Clearly indicate that it is unavailable.

Do not invent a replacement.

If there are no previous orders:

Show a useful empty state.

Example:

"No orders yet."

--------------------------------------------------
6. BUY AGAIN
--------------------------------------------------

Preserve the already-approved Reorder / Buy Again behavior.

Buy Again must:

- use exact historical product/variant/store identity
- use current price
- use current availability
- use current stock/orderability
- never restore historical price
- never silently substitute another variant
- respect the one-store cart rule

Do not rewrite the reorder backend.

Only improve its presentation/accessibility from Home where necessary.

--------------------------------------------------
7. GLOBAL SEARCH
--------------------------------------------------

Preserve the approved global customer search.

Customer should be able to search without first selecting a category.

Examples:

"rice"
"milk"
"eggs"
"apple"

Search should be understandable to a normal customer.

Category selection is optional.

Do not force:

Category → Subcategory → Product

before search.

Search results must remain store-scoped to the customer's selected/eligible shopping context.

--------------------------------------------------
8. SEARCH UX
--------------------------------------------------

Review:

- search field visibility
- typing behavior
- loading state
- empty results
- error state
- retry
- clearing search
- returning to Home
- returning from Product Details

Do not add unnecessary search filters.

Do not expose internal product identifiers.

Do not expose exact vendor stock.

Customer should see derived availability such as:

Available
Out of stock
Unavailable

--------------------------------------------------
9. CATEGORY UX
--------------------------------------------------

Categories should help discovery but must NOT be required for global search.

Keep category navigation simple.

If categories are displayed:

- make them easy to scroll
- avoid excessive horizontal overflow
- make selected state obvious
- preserve the current store context

Do not change Master Catalog taxonomy in this phase.

Do not create new master categories.

--------------------------------------------------
10. PRODUCT CARD UX
--------------------------------------------------

Review product cards for:

- product name
- brand where available
- variant/price presentation
- availability
- Add button
- Choose option button
- image
- spacing

Fix the previously identified issue where:

PRICE and ADD BUTTON overlap or visually collide.

The card must remain readable on:

- desktop
- tablet-sized browser
- mobile-sized browser

Do not allow long product names to break the layout.

Do not expose exact stock.

--------------------------------------------------
11. MULTIPLE VARIANT PRODUCTS
--------------------------------------------------

For products with multiple variants:

Example:

Rice:
500 g
1 kg
2 kg
5 kg

Customer must clearly understand that they need to choose a variant.

Preserve the already-approved Product Details behavior.

Do not create synthetic variants.

Do not change variant pricing/stock logic.

--------------------------------------------------
12. SINGLE-VARIANT PRODUCTS
--------------------------------------------------

For products with one purchasable variant:

Preserve the existing approved behavior where the variant can be selected automatically when appropriate.

Do not introduce an unnecessary extra variant-selection step.

--------------------------------------------------
13. OUT-OF-STOCK UX
--------------------------------------------------

Out-of-stock products should remain understandable.

Show:

- Out of stock
- Unavailable
- Disabled Add/Choose action as appropriate

Do not show exact stock.

Do not allow adding unavailable products to cart.

Do not silently hide every out-of-stock result unless the current product-discovery behavior already intentionally does so.

--------------------------------------------------
14. CART UX
--------------------------------------------------

The permanent Cart bottom navigation/menu must remain removed.

Preserve contextual cart behavior.

When the customer has items in cart:

show a clear contextual cart bar/action.

Example:

"Go to Cart — 2 Items"

The customer should be able to continue shopping.

Do not force the customer into the cart after every Add to Cart action.

Do not create a permanent duplicate cart navigation.

--------------------------------------------------
15. CART CONTEXT
--------------------------------------------------

The customer should always understand:

- which store the cart belongs to
- how many items are in cart
- when adding from another store would cause a store conflict

Preserve the existing one-store cart rule.

If adding from another store:

use the existing clear confirmation/replacement behavior.

Do not silently replace the cart.

--------------------------------------------------
16. BACK / RETURN NAVIGATION
--------------------------------------------------

Review all important navigation paths:

Home
→ Store
→ Category
→ Search
→ Product Details
→ Cart

Back should return the customer to the expected previous shopping context.

Examples:

Product Details → Back

should return to the previous product/search/store context rather than unexpectedly resetting Home.

Search → Product → Back

should preserve the search context where technically practical.

Cart → Continue Shopping

should return to the relevant store shopping context.

Do not introduce broken navigation stacks.

--------------------------------------------------
17. SCROLLING
--------------------------------------------------

Review customer pages for scrolling problems.

Verify:

- Home scrolls correctly
- Search results scroll
- Product lists scroll
- Product Details scroll
- Cart scrolls
- long product names/descriptions do not block actions
- bottom content is reachable
- contextual cart bar does not cover content

Do not introduce nested scrolling unnecessarily.

--------------------------------------------------
18. SCREEN RESOLUTION
--------------------------------------------------

Review customer web rendering at:

- desktop width
- tablet width
- mobile width

The UI must not:

- clip content
- overlap buttons
- hide important actions
- create horizontal scrolling unnecessarily
- push Add buttons outside cards

Do not redesign the entire visual system.

Make focused corrections.

--------------------------------------------------
19. STORE CONTEXT
--------------------------------------------------

The active store should always be clear when shopping.

Customer should know:

"Shopping from:
Development Store A"

or equivalent existing store presentation.

Do not allow products from another store to silently appear in the current store's catalog.

Preserve store isolation.

--------------------------------------------------
20. STORE SWITCHING
--------------------------------------------------

If the customer chooses another supermarket:

- explicitly change shopping store
- preserve existing cart conflict behavior
- do not silently merge carts from different stores

If cart contains another store's items:

show the existing confirmation before clearing/replacing the cart.

--------------------------------------------------
21. PINCODE / ELIGIBILITY
--------------------------------------------------

Preserve existing pincode/store eligibility logic.

Do not replace the current eligibility implementation.

Home should clearly communicate when the selected location has no eligible stores.

Do not expose internal eligibility calculations.

--------------------------------------------------
22. ORDER STATUS / DELIVERY
--------------------------------------------------

Customer order details must continue to display the approved Phase 7 delivery lifecycle:

READY
OUT_FOR_DELIVERY
DELIVERED

Do not create another tracking system.

Do not redesign delivery workflow.

Home may optionally surface relevant active orders if the existing architecture supports it, but do not add unnecessary notification infrastructure.

--------------------------------------------------
23. ACTIVE ORDER VISIBILITY
--------------------------------------------------

If a customer currently has an active order, consider a simple contextual section such as:

"Your current order"

with:

- order reference
- current status
- store
- View Order

Do not create a second order-management system.

Do not duplicate the entire Orders screen.

--------------------------------------------------
24. EMPTY STATES
--------------------------------------------------

Create useful empty states for:

No stores
No recent orders
No recent store
No search results
No cart items
No active orders

Empty states should be:

- clear
- short
- actionable where appropriate

Do not use fake products/orders/stores.

--------------------------------------------------
25. ERROR STATES
--------------------------------------------------

Review:

- API failure
- search failure
- store loading failure
- product loading failure
- cart failure
- checkout failure

Use clear customer-friendly messages.

Do not expose raw database/API errors.

Provide retry where appropriate.

--------------------------------------------------
26. LOADING STATES
--------------------------------------------------

Avoid blank screens while loading.

Use the existing loading/skeleton patterns where available.

Do not add excessive animations.

Keep the experience responsive.

--------------------------------------------------
27. CUSTOMER PRIVACY
--------------------------------------------------

Customer UI/API must continue NOT exposing:

- exact stock
- internal IDs
- vendor private information
- internal database timestamps
- SKU/external vendor codes unless explicitly customer-facing
- partner internal IDs

Preserve the Phase 2 privacy rules.

--------------------------------------------------
28. DO NOT CHANGE BUSINESS LOGIC
--------------------------------------------------

This phase is primarily UX.

Do NOT change:

- stock deduction timing
- stock restoration
- price authority
- variant identity
- order creation
- order status transitions
- vendor authorization
- delivery partner authorization
- Master Catalog business rules
- reorder identity rules
- one-store cart rule

If a UX issue exposes a backend bug, identify it and make the smallest safe correction only.

--------------------------------------------------
29. PERFORMANCE
--------------------------------------------------

Avoid unnecessary API calls.

Reuse existing data where practical.

Do not add polling to ordinary catalog/search screens unless an existing approved workflow requires it.

Do not introduce a new state-management system.

Do not introduce a new caching platform.
